This week we visited Billings Estate, where we spent time examining gravestones and learning about how archaeologists collect and record information. I found the experience pretty interesting. It became eye opening when we started discussing the ethics of recording information and how important it is to avoid spreading misinformation.

I was  surprised by the types of questions we were asked to question when looking at the gravestones. Some of the questions went into a lot more depth than I expected, which made me realize that there is a lot more to recording archaeological information than just writing down what you see. We also learned about photographing gravestones and how those photographs can be used to create 3D models. I thought this was a pretty interesting way to study historical objects digitally.

My biggest takeaway from this week was definitely the importance of being careful with the information we record. Before this visit, I hadn't really thought about the ethical side of archaeological documentation. I now have a better understanding of why it is important to recognize when you don't know something rather than filling in the gaps with guesses. Even small mistakes can become a problem when that information is shared or used by other people.
